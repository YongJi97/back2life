## The pitch (read naturally — don't recite it word for word)

---

### Opening

> "Hey, is this Santos? — Great. My name is [your name], I'm a web developer based
> here in the area. Sorry to bother you, I'll keep this quick —
> I was looking up barbers on Google and came across your shop on Booksy.
> Five-star rating, 44 reviews, really impressive.
> I noticed you don't have a website yet, and I actually built one for you
> as a demo — takes about 10 seconds to look at on your phone.
> Would you have a quick second to pull it up?"

*(If yes → give them the URL or send it via text/DM. If they hesitate → go to the value pitch.)*

---

### The value pitch (if they ask "why do I need one?")

> "Totally fair question. Here's the thing — when someone types
> 'barber Staten Island' or 'fade near me' into Google,
> Booksy and Yelp show up, but they're not YOUR page.
> People click those and see your competitors right next to you.
>
> A website gives you your own spot on Google that goes straight to you —
> your cuts, your prices, your booking link — no distractions.
> And with a 5.0 on Booksy, you've got the reviews to back it up.
> You just need a front door on the internet."

---

### Show the demo

> "I already built it out — it's got your services, your prices,
> the photos from your Google listing, and a button that sends people
> straight to your Booksy page to book. Took me a few hours.
> If you like it, it's yours. If not, no hard feelings at all."

*(Send the URL — your server IP:3005, or deploy it to a free Netlify link first for a cleaner demo link)*

---

### Pricing (after they've seen it)

> "So here's how I price it — it's a one-time build fee of **$300**,
> and then **$30 a month** which covers hosting, the domain, and
> I keep it updated for you. Price change, new photo, different hours —
> you just text me and I handle it.
>
> Most guys spend more than that on product every month."

---

### Handling common pushbacks

**"I already have Booksy / Instagram."**
> "Those are great, and they stay. The website actually links directly to
> your Booksy so people can book from there. It just means when someone
> Googles you — and they will — they find *you* first, not a directory."

**"I don't really use the internet for that."**
> "Your customers do though. Over 80% of people look up a business online
> before walking in, especially for something like a haircut where they
> want to see the work first. You've got great cuts — they just need
> somewhere to find them."

**"Let me think about it."**
> "Of course. Can I send you the link so you can show whoever you need to show?
> No commitment at all — I'd just love to know what you think of it."

**"How do I know you'll stick around for the $30/month?"**
> "That's a fair concern. I do this for a few local businesses already —
> you'd have my number, and if I ever can't deliver I'll hand you the
> files to take anywhere. You own everything."

**"Can you make it cheaper?"**
> "The $30/month I can't really move on — that's basically just hosting costs
> and my time. But if the $300 upfront is the issue, I can do $150 now
> and $150 after you see it live on a real domain. I'm not going anywhere."

---

### The close

> "So honestly, worst case you've got a free website to look at.
> Best case, you've got a new front door on Google for $30 a month.
> Want me to put it on a real link so you can share it with people
> and see how it feels?"

*(Goal of the first call: get them to look at the demo and agree to a follow-up, not close on the spot.)*

---

## Follow-up text to send right after the call

> Hey Santos, it was great talking to you.
> Here's the demo site I built for the shop: [your link]
> No pressure at all — just let me know what you think
> when you get a chance. — [your name]

---

## Notes

- **Don't lead with price.** Get them to see the demo first. Once they see
  their own shop looking professional, the $300 feels small.
- **Santos is your contact.** Based on Booksy he's the main (likely only)
  barber. You're probably talking to the decision-maker directly.
- **The Booksy angle is your opener.** A 5.0 with 44 reviews is a genuinely
  strong trust signal — lead with a compliment, it's not fake.
- **The real pitch is Google visibility.** Booksy is buried in search.
  A dedicated website with their address, name, and keywords can show up
  in the Google local pack. That's the thing that gets new walk-ins.
- **Have a live URL ready before you call.** Deploy to Netlify
  (drag and drop the folder at netlify.com/drop) for a free shareable
  link. A real URL is 10x more convincing than "I'll send you something later."
